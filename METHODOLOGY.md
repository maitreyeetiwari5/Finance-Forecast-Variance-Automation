# Methodology, Assumptions & Limitations

This document records how every number in this project is produced, what was assumed
along the way, and where the approach stops being reliable. The README gives the overview;
this is the detail a reviewer or auditor would ask for.

## 1. Data

All data is synthetic, generated by `generate_data.py` with a fixed random seed (42), so
every run produces identical numbers.

- **Scope:** 5 business units × 6 line items × 18 months (April 2025 – September 2026),
  540 budget/actual pairs.
- **Budgets and actuals:** each business unit has its own scale factor. Actuals follow
  budget with random noise, except in the scripted event months below.
- **Scripted anomalies (the ground truth):** six one-off events were built into the data
  so the detection logic could be tested against known answers.

| # | Business unit | Line item | Month | Actual vs. budget | Scenario |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Retail Sales | Opex: Marketing | Nov 2025 | +45% | Q4 campaign spend pulled forward |
| 2 | Retail Sales | Opex: Marketing | Jun 2026 | +30% | Mid-year campaign overspend |
| 3 | Digital Products | Revenue | Feb 2026 | −20% | Product launch delayed |
| 4 | Logistics | Opex: Other | Sep 2025 | +60% | Fuel cost spike |
| 5 | Enterprise Services | Headcount Cost | Apr 2026 | −25% | Planned hiring freeze |
| 6 | Customer Support | Opex: Salaries | Jan 2026 | +25% | Holiday overtime surge |

The same six events appear in `event_log.py` as analyst-maintained notes, and in
`validate_thresholds.py` as the answer key.

## 2. Forecast (`forecast.py`)

For each business unit and line item:

```
forecast[t]              = average(actual[t-1], actual[t-2], actual[t-3])
variance_vs_budget_pct   = (actual[t] − budget[t]) / budget[t]
variance_vs_forecast_pct = (actual[t] − forecast[t]) / forecast[t]
```

- The first three months of each series have no forecast (not enough history). They still
  carry budget variance, but are excluded from forecast comparisons.
- **Why a 3-month moving average:** anyone reviewing the output can recalculate the
  forecast by hand. A more complex model might be more accurate but could not be checked
  as easily.
- **Accuracy:** mean absolute percentage error (MAPE) of 7.0% across all months with a
  forecast.

## 3. Threshold calibration (`calibrate_thresholds.py`, `validate_thresholds.py`)

Each line item gets its own materiality threshold, learned from its own history:

```
threshold = median(|variance_vs_budget_pct|) + K × MAD_scaled
MAD_scaled = median(|x − median(x)|) × 1.4826
```

with a floor of 4%, so the steadiest line items do not get thresholds so tight that
normal noise trips them.

**Why median and MAD instead of mean and standard deviation:** the standard deviation is
inflated by the very outliers the thresholds exist to catch, which would loosen the
thresholds exactly where the anomalies are. Median and MAD are barely affected by a few
extreme values.

**Why K = 1.2 (and not the textbook 2.5):**

1. The first calibration used K = 2.5 (`calibrate_thresholds.py`, saved in
   `threshold_calibration.csv`). It looked statistically sound, but it set the Marketing
   threshold at 48.8% and caught only **4 of the 6** known events.
2. `validate_thresholds.py` then swept K downward and measured two things at each step:
   the share of all rows flagged, and how many known events were caught.

| K | Rows flagged | Known events caught |
|---|---|---|
| 2.5 | 1.7% | 4 / 6 |
| 2.0 | 4.1% | 5 / 6 |
| 1.5 | 7.8% | 5 / 6 |
| **1.2** | **12.8%** | **6 / 6** |
| 1.0 | 17.2% | 6 / 6 |

3. K = 1.2 is the lowest flag rate that still catches every known event, so it was chosen.

**Final thresholds** (these are the values hardcoded in `controls.py`; they come from the
formula above with K = 1.2):

| Line item | Original hardcoded threshold | K = 2.5 (rejected) | K = 1.2 (final) |
|---|---|---|---|
| COGS | 5% | 14.1% | 8.4% |
| Headcount Cost | 6% | 12.7% | 7.5% |
| Opex: Marketing | 15% | 48.8% | 28.5% |
| Opex: Other | 12% | 27.6% | 16.3% |
| Opex: Salaries | 5% | 12.1% | 7.1% |
| Revenue | 5% | 19.0% | 11.2% |

The original hardcoded thresholds flagged 34.8% of rows; the final calibrated thresholds
flag 12.8% while still catching all six known events.

## 4. Flagging (`controls.py`)

A row is flagged if **either** test breaches the line item's threshold:

- **Budget test:** |variance vs. budget| > threshold (a real over- or underspend against plan)
- **Forecast test:** |variance vs. forecast| > threshold (a surprise against recent trend,
  even when the budget itself was loose)

The two tests are checked separately, and `flag_reason` records which one fired and by how
much, so every flag can be traced to a specific number.

## 5. Commentary (`commentary.py`, `event_log.py`)

Commentary is **rule-based, not AI-generated**. For each flagged row:

1. Look up the business unit, line item, and month in the event log.
2. If a driver is on file, write the variance and the driver.
3. If not, write only the factual variance description and mark it
   "Driver: pending analyst review." The tool never invents a cause.

Result: 6 of 69 flagged rows matched a known driver and were explained; the other 63 were
described factually and routed for review.

## 6. Output data dictionary

**`forecast_vs_actual.csv`**

| Column | Meaning |
|---|---|
| business_unit, line_item, month | Series identifiers |
| budget, actual | Planned and actual amounts |
| forecast | 3-month moving average of prior actuals (blank for the first 3 months) |
| variance_vs_budget, variance_vs_budget_pct | actual − budget, and as a share of budget |
| variance_vs_forecast, variance_vs_forecast_pct | actual − forecast, and as a share of forecast |

**`flagged_variances.csv`** adds:

| Column | Meaning |
|---|---|
| threshold | Calibrated threshold for the line item |
| budget_breach / forecast_breach | Whether each test fired |
| flagged | True if either test fired |
| flag_reason | Plain-English description of which test fired and by how much |

**`commentary_draft.csv`**

| Column | Meaning |
|---|---|
| commentary | Drafted commentary line for the flagged row |
| has_known_driver | True if the driver came from the event log; False if routed for review |

**`threshold_calibration.csv`** is the output of the first (K = 2.5) calibration pass,
kept as a record of the rejected thresholds.

## 7. Assumptions

- Budget variance is measured as a percentage of budget, so thresholds scale with the size
  of each line item.
- A line item's variance behaves similarly across business units, so one threshold per
  line item is shared by all five units.
- The event log is complete and accurate for the events it contains.
- The six scripted events are a fair test of what a real anomaly looks like in size.

## 8. Limitations

- **Synthetic data.** Real ledgers have seasonality, reclasses, accruals, and one-time
  entries that this data does not. Results would need re-validation on real history.
- **K was chosen and validated on the same data.** Because the six known events were used
  both to choose K and to measure recall, the 6/6 result is in-sample. With real data,
  K should be chosen on one period and tested on a later one.
- **Thresholds are hardcoded in `controls.py`.** They match the K = 1.2 calibration, but
  if the data changes they must be recalculated and updated by hand. A production version
  would read them directly from the calibration step.
- **The moving average lags turning points.** A sustained trend change will be flagged for
  several months before the forecast catches up.
- **Commentary is only as good as the event log.** Drivers that nobody recorded will
  always show as "pending analyst review," which is the intended, safe behavior, but it
  means the tool does not reduce the need for analyst follow-up on unknown variances.

## 9. Reproducing the results

```bash
pip install -r requirements.txt
python generate_data.py
python forecast.py            # prints MAPE (7.0%)
python calibrate_thresholds.py
python validate_thresholds.py # prints the K sweep and the chosen K
python controls.py            # prints 69 of 540 rows flagged (12.8%)
python commentary.py          # prints 6 of 69 rows matched a known driver
python build_dashboard.py
python export_excel.py
```

With the fixed seed, every run reproduces the numbers in this document.
